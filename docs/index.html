Executive Summary

Spaceflight imposes complex abiotic stresses on plants, including microgravity, altered atmospheric gas exchange, and space radiation. However, the extent to which natural terrestrial genetic variation influences spaceflight adaptation remains poorly understood.

This project integrates 462 GWAS studies from the AraGWAS catalog (1,523,262 Bonferroni-significant SNP-trait associations across 2,029 accessions) with 13 NASA GeneLab spaceflight transcriptomics studies (22 ecotype/genotype comparisons). Through cross-study meta-analysis, machine learning, and knowledge graph modeling, we find that genes associated with terrestrial climate-adaptation and leaf ionome traits are enriched among spaceflight-responsive genes.

Status (14 Oct 2026): an evidence audit found that five of the 13 studies are dark-grown, that the code for the core analyses (scripts 01–19) is not in this repository, and that several earlier figures and claims rested on simulated data. Those have been removed; open questions are marked TODO in the manuscript.

Key Scientific Discoveries

  • Chromosome 4 Metabolite Locus (AT4G02820–AT4G02860): 29 of the 31 SNPs used for ecotype scoring, chosen as the highest-scoring associations in the AraGWAS catalog, lie in one 14 kb block associated with three LC-MS leaf metabolite features. The ecotype scores therefore rest largely on a single locus; it is not an ion-homeostasis locus.
  • Photorespiration Genes: 53 of 56 photorespiration genes (94.6%) are differentially expressed in spaceflight. Altered gas exchange around leaves in microgravity is one hypothesis for this; it was not tested here.
  • Latitudinal Clinal Variation: Predicted spaceflight response scores exhibit a statistically significant latitudinal gradient (Spearman ρ = -0.125, p = 8.97e-5), with northern accessions showing lower response distinctness.
  • Demographic Population Stratification: a regression on population group, latitude and elevation explains 34.9% of score variance; Asian accessions score lowest, and Spanish, relict and North Swedish accessions highest.

Repository & Publication Artifacts

This repository holds the results tables, figures, knowledge graph files, Docker and Conda environment specifications, the extended-analysis and figure scripts (20–27) and the LaTeX manuscript source. The scripts that produced the core results (01–19) were run in a Biomni workspace and are not in this repository, so those results cannot yet be reproduced from it.
