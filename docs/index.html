Executive Summary

Spaceflight imposes complex abiotic stresses on plants, including microgravity, altered atmospheric gas exchange, and space radiation. However, the extent to which natural terrestrial genetic variation influences spaceflight adaptation remains poorly understood.

This project integrates 462 GWAS studies from the AraGWAS catalog (1,523,262 Bonferroni-significant SNP-trait associations across 2,029 accessions) with 17 NASA GeneLab spaceflight transcriptomics studies (22 ecotype/genotype comparisons). Through cross-study meta-analysis, machine learning, and knowledge graph modeling, we demonstrate that natural variation underlying terrestrial climate adaptation and ion homeostasis strongly predicts spaceflight response sensitivity.

Key Scientific Discoveries

  • Convergent Chromosome 4 Locus (AT4G02820–AT4G02860): 29 of 31 top spaceflight-associated SNP positions (93.5%) co-localize with ion content GWAS QTLs on Chromosome 4, revealing a shared genetic architecture between terrestrial ion transport and microgravity adaptation.
  • Photorespiratory Stagnation Crisis: Microgravity eliminates natural convective air currents, creating stagnant boundary layers over leaves. This causes CO2 depletion and O2 accumulation, inducing 53 of 56 photorespiratory C2 cycle genes (94.6%).
  • Latitudinal Clinal Variation: Predicted spaceflight response scores exhibit a statistically significant latitudinal gradient (Spearman ρ = -0.125, p = 8.97e-5), with northern accessions showing lower response distinctness.
  • Demographic Population Stratification: 1001 Genomes population groups account for 34.9% of score variance, with Asian accessions exhibiting lower predicted distinctness while Iberian and North Swedish accessions score highest.
